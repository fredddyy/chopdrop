## DESIGN NOTES

### The split-screen is the entire personality.

Every comparison slide uses the same split layout. Left = grey, exhausting, crowded. Right = clean, spacious, calm. The visual contrast does 70% of the persuasion before anyone reads a word.

### Never name a competitor.

"The other way" is all you need. Everyone fills in the blank with whichever app hurt them most recently. This way you get the engagement ("lmaooo this is Chowdeck/Glovo/Bolt") without the legal risk or the pettiness.

### The slides people will screenshot:

- **Slide 2** (₦4,500 on delivery) — people tag their office managers
- **Slide 4** (the empty right side — "That's it. That's the slide.") — people share this for the comedy
- **Slide 5** (₦18,000/month math) — this is the "wait what" moment that gets forwarded to HR
- **Slide 7** (the wrong order checklist with "Regret") — pure relatability
- **Slide 9** (the honest slide) — this is the one people put on their Stories with "this is so true"

### Swipe rate optimization:

- Slide 1 hooks with a relatable statement (not a logo)
- Each slide ends with an implicit "it gets worse/better" — people swipe to see the next comparison
- The split format creates a pattern that's satisfying to swipe through
- Slide 9 breaks the pattern intentionally (no split, just text) — this signals "this is the real talk" and slows people down before the CTA
