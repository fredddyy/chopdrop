## 1. WHO WE ARE

### Brand essence (one line)

**ChopDrop makes office lunch effortless.**

### Brand story

In Lagos, ordering lunch for a group of office workers is a daily war. Someone has to collect orders. Someone has to chase payments. Someone has to call the food person. Delivery fees eat into everyone's budget. The food arrives late, cold, or wrong. And tomorrow, the whole cycle starts again.

ChopDrop exists because that cycle is broken and nobody was fixing it.

We built a system — not an app, a system — where everyone in an office picks their own meal, pays on their own phone, and food arrives together at a set time. One delivery. Zero fees. Zero drama.

We started with office lunch in Ikeja. But ChopDrop is really about something bigger: removing the friction from any moment where a group of people needs to eat together. Offices today. Breakfasts, events, estates, and campuses tomorrow.

### What we are

- A group food ordering system for workplaces
- A logistics product that batches 20–30 meals into one efficient delivery
- A tool that eliminates coordination overhead for teams that eat together

### What we are NOT

- A food delivery app (we don't deliver to individuals at home)
- A restaurant (we don't cook anything)
- A marketplace (we don't show 400 restaurants and hope you pick one)
- A meal plan subscription (you order what you want, when you want)

## 2. BRAND PERSONALITY

### If ChopDrop were a person

She's the sharp, put-together colleague everyone trusts to organize things. Not the loud one — the effective one. She doesn't overcomplicate. She doesn't do drama. She gets lunch sorted before 10 AM and never mentions it again because it wasn't a big deal.

She's warm but efficient. Friendly but not trying too hard. She dresses well but doesn't overdress. She knows Lagos inside out — the traffic, the food spots, the office politics — but she doesn't complain about it, she navigates it.

She's 27. She works in tech or consulting. She says "sorted" a lot.

### Brand voice characteristics

| Trait | What it sounds like | What it DOESN'T sound like |
|-------|--------------------|-----------------------------|
| **Confident** | "₦0 delivery. One drop. Everyone eats." | "We're excited to announce our revolutionary platform!" |
| **Warm** | "Work is stressful. Food shouldn't be." | "Optimize your team's nutritional logistics." |
| **Direct** | "Pick. Pay. Done." | "Leveraging our seamless end-to-end solution..." |
| **Knowing** | "You've been scrolling for 11 minutes." | "Did you know that many people struggle with..." |
| **Cheeky (sparingly)** | "Buy yourself something nice." | "LMAOOO 😂🤣💀💀💀" |
| **Nigerian** | "Bros, how much is delivery?" | "Yo fam check this out bruh" |

### Words we use

```
sorted          → our favorite word
drop            → how we describe a delivery (not "shipment" or "order fulfillment")
pick            → what users do (not "select" or "choose from our curated selection")
ChopDropper     → the office person who distributes meals
office          → where we deliver (not "workplace" or "corporate environment")
everyone        → our audience is collective, not individual
hot food        → what arrives (not "fresh cuisine" or "gourmet meals")
```

### Words we never use

```
seamless        → startup cliché
revolutionary   → we're not curing cancer, we're delivering rice
game-changer    → see above
leverage        → this is food, not finance
disrupt         → we serve lunch, we don't disrupt it
empower         → no
synergy         → absolutely not
at scale        → just say "for a lot of people"
```

## 3. VISUAL IDENTITY

### 3.1 Logo

**The mark:** A coral teardrop (a "drop") with a white smile curve inside. It's friendly, recognizable, and works at any size.

**The wordmark:** "ChopDrop" in Manrope ExtraBold (800), tracking -0.03em.

**Logo versions:**

```
FULL LOGO (mark + wordmark):
- Primary use: website headers, brand cards, CTA slides, documents
- Minimum width: 120px digital, 30mm print

MARK ONLY (teardrop):
- Secondary use: app icon, favicons, watermarks, social avatars
- Minimum size: 24px digital, 8mm print

WORDMARK ONLY (text):
- Rare use: when the mark is already established in context
- Never use the wordmark alone as the primary logo
```

**Logo clear space:**

The mark needs breathing room equal to the width of the smile curve on all sides. Never crowd it.

```
        ╭─────╮
   ═══  │  ◡  │  ═══
        ╰─────╯
   ═══     ═══     ═══

   ═══ = minimum clear space
```

**Logo don'ts:**

- Don't stretch or squeeze
- Don't rotate
- Don't add drop shadows, glows, or effects
- Don't place on a clashing or busy background without contrast
- Don't recreate or approximate — always use the original file
- Don't put the mark inside a box, circle, or other container shape

### 3.3 Typography

**Primary typeface: Manrope**

```
Usage:        Headlines, display text, big numbers, logo wordmark
Weights:      700 (Bold), 800 (ExtraBold)
Tracking:     -0.03em (slightly tight — creates confidence)
Style:        Geometric sans-serif. Modern, clean, slightly rounded.
              Feels technical but approachable.

Sizes (mobile):
  Hero:       28–32px
  Section:    20–24px
  Card title: 14–16px

Sizes (desktop):
  Hero:       48–64px
  Section:    28–36px
  Card title: 16–20px

Sizes (social/print at 1080px canvas):
  Hero:       120–160pt
  Headline:   48–64pt
  Body:       20–24pt
```

**Secondary typeface: DM Sans**

```
Usage:        Body text, descriptions, labels, UI text, captions
Weights:      400 (Regular), 500 (Medium), 700 (Bold)
Tracking:     Normal (0)
Style:        Clean, geometric, excellent readability at small sizes.
              Pairs perfectly with Manrope — same geometric DNA
              but softer for long reading.

Line height:  1.5–1.6x for body text
              1.2–1.3x for labels and UI elements
```

**Type pairing rules:**

```
✓  Manrope 800 headline + DM Sans 400 body     (most common)
✓  Manrope 700 card title + DM Sans 500 label   (UI elements)
✓  Manrope 800 big number + DM Sans 400 caption  (stat slides)

✗  Never use Manrope for body paragraphs (too heavy)
✗  Never use DM Sans for hero headlines (too light)
✗  Never mix more than these 2 typefaces anywhere
```

**Fallback stack (web):**

```css
--font-display: 'Manrope', system-ui, -apple-system, sans-serif;
--font-body: 'DM Sans', system-ui, -apple-system, sans-serif;
```

### 3.4 Iconography

**Style:** Outline icons, 1.5–2px stroke weight, rounded caps and joins.

```
Characteristics:
- Monoline (consistent stroke weight)
- Rounded stroke caps (strokeLinecap: round)
- Rounded stroke joins (strokeLinejoin: round)
- Simple geometry — never more than 2-3 shapes per icon
- No fills except for emphasis states (active nav items)

Standard size:  20×20px in UI, 16×16px in compact contexts
Touch target:   Minimum 44×44px for interactive icons

Color:
- Default: Ink #1A1A1A or Mid Grey #9CA3AF
- Active: Coral #F05F4B
- On dark: White #FFFFFF
- Never multicolor. Icons are single-color always.
```

### 3.5 Corner radius system

```
Small:    8px     (buttons, badges, input fields)
Medium:   12px    (cards, images, containers)
Large:    16px    (modal corners, large cards)
XL:       20-24px (hero cards, feature sections)
Full:     9999px  (pills, tags, circular buttons)
```

Everything has rounded corners. Nothing has sharp 0px corners. ChopDrop is friendly, not aggressive.

---

### 3.6 Shadow system

```
Subtle:   0 1px 2px rgba(0,0,0,0.04)         → cards at rest
Light:    0 1px 3px rgba(0,0,0,0.04),
          0 4px 16px rgba(0,0,0,0.04)         → elevated cards
Medium:   0 2px 4px rgba(0,0,0,0.07),
          0 8px 24px rgba(0,0,0,0.06)         → hover states, modals
Strong:   0 4px 20px rgba(0,0,0,0.15)         → floating elements, toasts

Coral:    0 2px 10px rgba(240,95,75,0.3)      → coral buttons only
Green:    0 4px 20px rgba(15,64,48,0.35)      → green CTAs, premium cards
```

Shadows are always warm-toned (rgba black, never blue-tinted). They should feel like natural light falling on paper, not digital glow.

### 3.7 Photography direction

**Food photography:**

```
Lighting:     Natural daylight or warm artificial. Never cold/blue.
Angle:        45° overhead (the "eating" angle). Not flat top-down, not eye-level.
Mood:         Abundance, warmth, appetite. Should make you hungry.
Subjects:     Real Nigerian food — jollof, amala, fried rice, pepper soup.
              NOT sushi, NOT avocado toast, NOT stock photo burgers.
Context:      Food in real containers/packs on office desks.
              NOT restaurant plating on white tablecloths.
Editing:      Warm white balance (+5-10%). Slightly boosted saturation (+10%).
              Never filtered. Never desaturated. Never cold-toned.
```

**People photography:**

```
Subjects:     Real Lagos professionals. Smart casual. Diverse.
              20s–40s. The people you actually see in Ikeja offices.
Environment:  Real offices — desks, corridors, reception areas.
              NOT co-working stock photos. NOT Silicon Valley campuses.
Expression:   Natural. Candid over posed. Smiling over serious.
              The vibe of "lunchtime just happened and it went well."
Diversity:    Reflect Lagos. Different ethnicities, genders, ages.
              No token casting — genuine representation.
```

**Imagery we never use:**

```
✗  Western stock photography (people who clearly aren't Nigerian)
✗  Overhead flatlay of a single perfect meal (too Instagram food-blogger)
✗  Dark, moody food photography (we're not a fine dining restaurant)
✗  Corporate handshake photos (we're not a consulting firm)
✗  Illustrated characters or mascots (we use real imagery)
```

## 7. BRAND HIERARCHY — WHERE THE LOGO GOES

```
DOMINANCE LEVELS:

Level 1 (logo dominates):
  - App splash screen
  - CTA slide (last slide of carousel)
  - Email headers
  - Business cards
  - Brand videos (closing frame)

Level 2 (logo present but secondary):
  - App header (small mark + wordmark)
  - Website nav
  - Social post hook slides (small mark in corner)
  - Flyer headers

Level 3 (logo absent or minimal):
  - In-app screens (the UI IS the brand)
  - Social media content slides (middle of carousel)
  - Operational documents
```

The rule: the deeper someone is in your product or content, the less logo they need. The brand is carried by the colors, type, and voice — not by stamping the logo everywhere.

## 8. THE NON-NEGOTIABLES

These rules are never broken, regardless of context:

```
1. Cream, not white. Our backgrounds are #FAFAF8, not #FFFFFF.
   This warmth is the single most distinctive thing about our visual identity.

2. Coral is for one thing per screen. If everything is coral, nothing is.
   One CTA. One price. One highlight. That's it.

3. Real Nigerian food. Never Western stock photography.
   Our audience eats jollof and amala, not acai bowls.

4. Manrope for display, DM Sans for body. No exceptions.
   No Poppins, no Montserrat, no Helvetica, no Arial.

5. Rounded corners on everything. We are friendly, not aggressive.
   Minimum 8px radius. Nothing has sharp corners.

6. The voice is human. Write like a person, not a brand.
   If you wouldn't say it to a colleague at lunch, don't write it.

7. The tagline is "Meals for offices, sorted." Not "sorted!" with
   an exclamation mark. We're calm. We don't shout.

8. ₦ not NGN, not "Naira". Always the symbol. Always with commas
   for thousands. ₦1,600 not ₦1600 not 1600 Naira.
```
