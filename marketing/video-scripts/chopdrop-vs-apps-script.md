## VOICEOVER NOTES

- **Voice:** Female, 25–32. She sounds like the person in the office who everyone asks "where should we order from?" — knowledgeable, direct, a little bit cheeky but never rude. She's not attacking competitors. She's just... saying what everyone already thinks.
- **Key tone rule:** We're not bitter about other apps. We respect them. We just solve a different problem. The line "those apps are great for Saturday night suya" is genuine — not sarcastic. We're carving our lane, not trashing theirs.
- **Pace:** Scene 1 is measured, almost tired (matching the frustration). Scene 3 is rhythmic and punchy (the comparisons should feel like a beat drop). Scene 4 slows down to land the emotional close.

## MUSIC DIRECTION

- **0:00–0:15 (Truth):** Minimal. Just a low bass pulse and the sound of phone notifications, scrolling, a distant office hum. Almost ASMR-documentary.
- **0:15–0:22 (Difference):** One clean chord. A breath. Then a confident Afrobeats kick comes in — not loud, just present.
- **0:22–0:40 (Breakdown):** The beat builds. Each comparison lands on a beat. Snap sounds on the transitions. Rhythmic, satisfying.

- **0:50–0:60 (Close):** Resolves to a single warm tone. Logo lands in near-silence. Confident. Done.

## WHY THIS SCRIPT WORKS

### 1. We never mention a competitor by name.
We show the *experience* — the scrolling, the fees, the waiting. Everyone recognizes it. Nobody can accuse us of attacking a brand.

### 2. We position by subtraction, not addition.
We don't say "we have more features." We say "we removed the things that waste your time." No 400 restaurants. No delivery fees. No guessing when food arrives.

### 3. We own a specific lane.
"Built for offices. Not for everything." — this is the key line. We're not trying to be Chowdeck. We're not trying to deliver suya at midnight. We solve one problem extremely well.

### 4. The "Saturday suya" line is the strategic anchor.
It acknowledges competitors without disrespecting them. It says: "they're good at what they do, but what they do isn't what you need at 12 PM on a Wednesday." This is the line people will quote.

### 5. The comparison format is algorithm-friendly.
Split-screen comparisons perform extremely well on Reels/TikTok. They're inherently shareable. People screenshot them. They drive comments ("this is so true" / "lmao the 35 mins part").
